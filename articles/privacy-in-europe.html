Privacy in Europe

July 27, 2026 12 min read

The European Union has long positioned itself as a global leader in digital privacy. From the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) to the ePrivacy Directive, Brussels has built a regulatory framework that influences privacy policy worldwide. But recent developments suggest that Europe's commitment to privacy is being tested.

The Chat Control Proposal

In 2022, the European Commission proposed a regulation commonly referred to as "Chat Control" — a legislative effort to combat child sexual abuse material (CSAM) by requiring messaging platforms to scan user communications. The proposal has evolved through several iterations, but the core tension remains: how to protect children without dismantling the encryption that protects everyone.

"The Chat Control proposal represents one of the most significant threats to encrypted communications in the Western world." — European Digital Rights (EDRi)

The proposal has been met with widespread opposition from privacy advocates, technology companies, and even some EU member states. Critics argue that client-side scanning — the technical mechanism proposed — would fundamentally undermine the security of end-to-end encrypted systems.

The GDPR Legacy

To understand the current debate, it helps to understand what came before. The GDPR, which took effect in May 2018, established a comprehensive framework for data protection that has become a global benchmark. Key provisions include:

  • Data minimization — Collect only what you need, keep it only as long as necessary.
  • Purpose limitation — Use data only for the purpose for which it was collected.
  • Consent — Obtain explicit, informed consent before processing personal data.
  • Right to erasure — The "right to be forgotten" allows individuals to request data deletion.
  • Data portability — Users can transfer their data between services.

The GDPR's influence extends far beyond Europe. Companies around the world have adopted GDPR-compliant practices, and several countries — including Brazil, Japan, and South Korea — have modelled their own privacy laws on the GDPR framework.

The Encryption Debate

Encryption is the technical foundation of digital privacy. Without strong encryption, communications, financial transactions, and even stored data are vulnerable to interception. The EU's current debate over encryption mirrors a global conversation:

On one side are law enforcement agencies who argue that encryption hampers criminal investigations. On the other are privacy advocates, technologists, and human rights organisations who maintain that encryption is essential for safety, democracy, and human rights.

The technical reality is that there is no way to create a "backdoor" that only the "good guys" can use. Any vulnerability in an encrypted system is a vulnerability for everyone — criminals, nation-states, and ordinary users alike.

What's at Stake

The outcome of Europe's privacy debate has global implications. If the EU mandates client-side scanning or weakens encryption, other jurisdictions are likely to follow. Conversely, if Europe maintains its commitment to strong privacy protections, it sets a standard that the rest of the world can aspire to.

For now, the situation remains uncertain. The Chat Control proposal is still being negotiated, and civil society organisations continue to push back. What is clear is that the stakes could not be higher: the future of private digital communication hangs in the balance.

What You Can Do

If you are concerned about privacy in Europe, here are some practical steps:

  • Use end-to-end encrypted messaging apps like Signal.
  • Support organisations like EDRi and the Electronic Frontier Foundation.
  • Contact your MEP (Member of the European Parliament) about privacy legislation.
  • Self-host your own services where possible.
  • Stay informed — follow privacy news and understand the issues.

Privacy isn't optional. It's a fundamental right that requires active defence. The decisions being made in Brussels today will shape the digital landscape for generations to come.
