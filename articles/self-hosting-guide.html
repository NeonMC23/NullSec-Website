Self Hosting

The Complete Self-Hosting Guide for 2026

July 27, 202620 min read
Quick Summary

Self-hosting puts you back in control of your data. This guide covers everything from choosing hardware (Raspberry Pi, Intel NUC, or old laptop) to essential services (Nextcloud, Bitwarden, Pi-hole) and security considerations. No third party has access to your files, and you are not subject to the data practices of big tech companies.

#SelfHosting #Nextcloud #Privacy

Self-hosting is the practice of running your own services on hardware you control. Instead of relying on Google Drive, Dropbox, or Gmail, you host your own file storage, email, and applications on infrastructure that belongs to you. This guide covers everything you need to know to get started with self-hosting in 2026.

Why Self-Host?

Self-hosting gives you full control over your data. No third party has access to your files, emails, or communications. You are not subject to the privacy policies, terms of service changes, or data retention practices of large technology companies. In an era of increasing surveillance and data monetisation, self-hosting is one of the most effective ways to protect your digital sovereignty.

Hardware Options

You can self-host on a Raspberry Pi, an old laptop, a dedicated server, or a virtual private server (VPS). For beginners, we recommend starting with a Raspberry Pi 5 (4GB or 8GB model) or an Intel NUC. These devices are energy-efficient, quiet, and powerful enough for most self-hosted services. For more advanced setups, consider a refurbished enterprise server from Dell PowerEdge or HP ProLiant series.

Essential Services to Self-Host

  • Nextcloud — File storage, calendar, contacts, and collaborative document editing. A complete Google Workspace replacement.
  • Bitwarden — Self-hosted password manager. All your passwords encrypted and under your control.
  • Pi-hole — Network-wide ad blocking and DNS filtering. Runs on any Linux system.
  • Jellyfin — Free and open source media streaming server. Alternative to Plex and Emby.
  • Home Assistant — Open source home automation platform. Control all your smart devices locally.
  • Vaultwarden — Lightweight Bitwarden-compatible server written in Rust. Ideal for low-power hardware.

Security Considerations

Self-hosting comes with significant responsibility. You must keep your software updated, use strong and unique passwords, enable firewalls, configure automatic security updates, and implement proper backup strategies. We recommend using Tailscale or WireGuard for secure remote access to your services, rather than exposing them directly to the internet.

A backup strategy is non-negotiable. Follow the 3-2-1 rule: three copies of your data, on at least two different media, with one copy off-site. Tools like BorgBackup, Restic, or rsync can automate this process.
